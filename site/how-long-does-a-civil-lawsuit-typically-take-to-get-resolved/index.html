How Long Does a Civil Lawsuit Take in New York?

By  Akiva Shapiro, Esq. Published 2018 · Last reviewed August 2026

Most civil lawsuits in New York take between one and three years to resolve from the day the case is filed — but the range is wide. A straightforward dispute can settle in a few months, while a complex case that goes all the way through discovery and trial can take three years or more. The large majority of civil cases settle before they ever reach a courtroom, which often shortens the timeline considerably.

A Realistic Timeline for a New York Civil Lawsuit

There is no single answer, because "a civil lawsuit" covers everything from a small contract dispute to complex commercial litigation. As a general guide in New York:

  • Simple cases that settle early: a few months to a year.
  • Typical contested cases: roughly one to two years.
  • Complex cases (multiple parties, extensive discovery, expert witnesses, or an eventual trial): two to four years or more.

What drives the number is not the size of the dispute so much as how hard it is fought, how much information has to be exchanged, and how backed up the particular court is.

The Stages of a Civil Lawsuit — and How Long Each Takes

Understanding where the time goes helps set realistic expectations:

  • Pleadings (1–3 months): the case begins when the plaintiff files and serves a summons and complaint; the defendant then has a set period to answer.
  • Discovery (6 months to 2 years): this is almost always the longest stage. Both sides exchange documents, answer written questions, and take depositions. Discovery disputes are the most common reason cases drag.
  • Motions (varies): either side may ask the court to decide part or all of the case — for example, a motion for summary judgment — which can add months while the court considers it.
  • Note of issue & trial readiness: once discovery is complete, the case is certified ready and placed on the trial calendar.
  • Trial: reaching an available trial date can itself take many months because of court backlogs, and the trial and any post-trial motions add more time.

What Makes a Case Take Longer — or Settle Faster

Several factors push the timeline in either direction:

  • Complexity: more parties, more claims, and more documents mean longer discovery.
  • Court congestion: some New York courts carry heavier caseloads than others, which affects how quickly motions are decided and trials scheduled.
  • Cooperation between the parties: when both sides exchange information promptly, the case moves; when they fight over every request, it stalls.
  • Willingness to settle: a realistic settlement posture on both sides can end a case in months rather than years.
  • Appeals: if the losing side appeals, that can add a year or more on top of everything else.

Why Most Civil Cases Settle Before Trial

The vast majority of civil lawsuits never reach a verdict. Trials are expensive, time-consuming, and unpredictable — and both sides know it. Settlement lets each party control the outcome, cap the cost, and end the stress on a known timeline. A good attorney prepares every case as if it will be tried, precisely because that readiness is what produces a strong settlement. Being ready for trial and settling are not opposites; the first is what makes the second work in your favor.

How to Keep Your Case Moving

You can't control the court's calendar, but you can influence your side of the case:

  • Respond quickly when your attorney asks for documents or information.
  • Keep records organized from the start.
  • Be realistic about goals so settlement opportunities aren't missed.
  • Work with counsel who stays on top of deadlines and keeps the case on the court's schedule rather than drifting.

Just as important is not missing the deadline to sue in the first place. New York's statutes of limitations vary by claim — for example, generally three years for many personal-injury claims and six years for breach of a written contract — and once the deadline passes, the right to sue is usually lost. If you think you may have a claim, it's worth confirming the deadline early.

Key Takeaways

  • Most New York civil lawsuits resolve in one to three years; simple cases can settle in months.
  • Discovery is usually the longest stage — often six months to two years.
  • Complexity, court backlog, and how hard the case is fought matter more than the dollar amount.
  • The large majority of civil cases settle before trial; preparing for trial is what produces good settlements.
  • Watch the statute of limitations — miss the deadline to sue and the claim is usually lost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take for a civil lawsuit to settle?

Settlement can happen at almost any stage — sometimes within a few months, often after discovery is substantially complete, and occasionally on the courthouse steps before trial. Many New York cases settle in the one-to-two-year range, but a case with motivated parties and clear facts can settle much sooner.

How long does discovery take in a New York lawsuit?

Discovery is usually the longest phase, commonly running from about six months to two years. The timeline depends on how many documents and witnesses are involved and how cooperative the parties are. Discovery disputes that require the court to intervene are a frequent cause of delay.

Can I speed up my civil lawsuit?

You can't control the court's calendar, but you can help your case move by responding promptly to your attorney, keeping records organized, being realistic about settlement, and working with counsel who actively manages deadlines. Cases that drift usually do so because information isn't exchanged on time.

How long do I have to file a civil lawsuit in New York?

It depends on the type of claim. New York's statutes of limitations vary — for example, many personal-injury claims must generally be filed within three years, and breach of a written contract generally within six years. Because deadlines differ and exceptions exist, confirm the specific limit for your claim with an attorney before it runs out.

Do most civil cases go to trial?

No. The large majority of civil lawsuits settle or are otherwise resolved before trial. Trials are costly and unpredictable, so both sides usually have strong incentives to settle — especially when a case has been thoroughly prepared.
