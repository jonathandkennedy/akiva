How Long Does It Take A Court To Grant A Guardianship?

By  Akiva Shapiro, Esq. Published 2018 · Last reviewed August 2026

In New York, an uncontested Article 81 guardianship typically takes roughly two to four months from filing the petition to the guardian actually receiving authority to act. The statute pushes courts to schedule the hearing within about a month of filing, but real-world timelines depend on court calendars, the court evaluator's investigation, and post-hearing paperwork. Contested cases, where family members fight over whether a guardian is needed or who it should be, can stretch to many months or longer. In true emergencies, a court can appoint a temporary guardian much faster.

The Typical Article 81 Timeline

A guardianship case moves through predictable stages, and each one takes time:

  • Preparing and filing the petition. Gathering medical facts, financial information, and specific examples of incapacity can take a few weeks before anything is filed.
  • The hearing date. Article 81 directs the court to hold a hearing promptly after the order to show cause is signed, generally within about 28 days, though busy calendars can push it.
  • The court evaluator's work. The evaluator must interview the alleged incapacitated person and others, then file a report before the hearing.
  • Decision and commission. After the judge rules, the order must be signed, the guardian completes training and files a designation, and a bond may be required. Only then is the commission issued, which is the document banks and agencies accept.

What Slows A Guardianship Down

The biggest variable is conflict. If a sibling objects to your appointment, if the alleged incapacitated person contests the case through counsel, or if there are allegations of financial abuse, the court may order discovery, adjourn hearings, or hold what amounts to a trial. That can add months.

Practical delays matter too: incomplete petitions get bounced, service on required family members takes time, evaluators need access to a resistant elder, and post-hearing steps like bonding and filing the commission are often where weeks quietly disappear. An experienced guardianship attorney keeps these mechanical steps moving.

Emergency And Temporary Guardianship

When a person is in immediate danger, being financially exploited, facing an urgent medical decision, or about to lose their home, New York courts can appoint a temporary guardian while the main case proceeds. The judge can grant specific emergency powers within days of filing in a compelling case.

Emergency relief is not automatic. You must show the court concrete, imminent harm, not just general concern. If your family is facing that kind of situation on Long Island or elsewhere in New York, building the emergency showing into the initial papers is critical.

Key Takeaways

  • Uncontested New York guardianships commonly take about two to four months start to finish.
  • The law pushes for a prompt hearing, generally within about 28 days of the signed order to show cause.
  • Contested cases with family objections can take many months.
  • Post-hearing steps like the bond and commission add time even after the judge says yes.
  • Courts can appoint a temporary guardian within days when there is proof of imminent harm.
