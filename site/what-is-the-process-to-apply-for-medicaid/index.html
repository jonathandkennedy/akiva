What Is The Process To Apply For Medicaid?

By  Akiva Shapiro, Esq. Published 2018 · Last reviewed August 2026

Applying for Medicaid in New York involves four basic stages: determining which program you need (community Medicaid for home care or institutional Medicaid for a nursing home), gathering documentation of your identity, income, and assets, filing the application with your local Department of Social Services, and responding to the agency's follow-up requests until a decision is issued. For seniors seeking long-term care, the smart process starts earlier, with a planning review, because the structure of your assets on the day you apply largely determines what you keep.

Step One: Identify The Right Program And Timing

New York Medicaid is not one program. Community Medicaid covers care at home, aides, personal care, and managed long-term care, and its financial rules are currently more forgiving on past transfers. Institutional (chronic-care) Medicaid covers nursing homes and imposes a five-year look-back on asset transfers, with penalties for gifts made in that window.

Where you are in that timeline changes strategy completely. Applying for home care, entering a nursing home next month, or planning years ahead are three different projects. Sorting this out first prevents the most expensive mistakes, like filing a nursing-home application while a recent gift sits unexplained in the record.

Step Two: Assemble The Documentation

Medicaid decisions rise and fall on paperwork. Expect to produce:

  • Proof of identity, age, citizenship or immigration status, and New York residence.
  • Income proof: Social Security award letters, pension statements, IRA distributions.
  • Asset records: bank, brokerage, and retirement statements; deeds; life insurance; for nursing-home cases, up to five years of statements.
  • Health insurance cards and, for long-term care, medical documentation supporting the need for services.

Gaps and unexplained withdrawals invite denials or demands for more records. Organizing this file before submission is where much of an elder law attorney's value shows up.

Step Three: File, Follow Up, And Protect Your Rights

Long-term care applications for seniors are generally filed with the local Department of Social Services in your county, in Nassau and Suffolk, the county DSS, while many younger applicants enroll through the state marketplace. After filing, a caseworker reviews the file and typically sends requests for additional documentation on short deadlines. Missing one can sink the application.

The agency then issues a written decision. If you are approved, watch the budget letter for errors in income calculations or spend-down amounts. If you are denied, or your penalty period is miscalculated, you have the right to request a Medicaid fair hearing before an administrative law judge, and many denials are reversed there. Deadlines to request a hearing are strict, so act on a denial immediately rather than reapplying blindly.

Key Takeaways

  • Community Medicaid and nursing-home Medicaid have different rules; pick your track first.
  • Nursing-home applications require roughly five years of financial records.
  • Applications are filed with your county Department of Social Services for long-term care.
  • Respond to every agency document request; missed deadlines cause denials.
  • Wrongful denials can be challenged at a Medicaid fair hearing with strict time limits.
